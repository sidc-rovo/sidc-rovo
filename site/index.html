The question

Why can solopreneurs ship at lightning speed while organizations generate everything and ship nothing?

The answer isn't talent and it isn't tooling. Both sides have the same models now. The difference is what happens after generation - the checking, the deciding, the getting-it-agreed. A solo builder does that in their head in seconds. An organization does it across six teams, four tools, and three weeks.

So the interesting work is no longer making the model produce something. It's making the verification and coordination cheap enough that shipping stops being the bottleneck. That's what I build.

Three bets

Each one is a negation and a replacement. If any of the three breaks, most of what I'm building breaks with it.

1. The future of AI is coordination, not generation.

Getting a good answer out of a model is now easy and nearly free. Getting many agents, systems, and people to converge on one answer and finish the job is neither. The scarce thing is agreement, not output.

2. The future of health AI is evidence, not prediction.

A confident prediction that nobody can audit is a liability, not a product. In health the deliverable isn't the score - it's the traceable chain of evidence that lets a clinician, a regulator, or a patient act on it.

3. The future of software is dual-intent.

Software now has two classes of user: people who need context and affordances, and agents that need structure and contracts. Build for one and you break the other. Dual-intent means designing both surfaces from the start.

Field notes

Short pieces on how AI changes the way work gets built. Written while building, not after. They contradict each other occasionally.

  • The Enterprise Refactor - Software is moving from deterministic code to intent. When that happens the bottleneck moves too: out of generation and into verification. Most enterprise AI programs are still optimizing the part that's already solved.
  • The App That Builds Itself - Chatbot, agent, or tool? It isn't a taste question. The right interface falls out of the kind of work being done: how reversible it is, how often it repeats, and how much the user already knows.
  • AI Won't Simplify Work - The promise was less complexity. The reality is displaced complexity. The goal worth aiming at is returning human attention to the complexity that actually deserves it, and automating the rest out of sight.
  • The Value Function - A way to balance three things that pull against each other: voice, evidence, and execution. Over-index on any one and the work either doesn't land, doesn't hold up, or doesn't ship.
